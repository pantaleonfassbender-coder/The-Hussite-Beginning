## Mlad. Arrest 1 A rumour and a haycart
EN: When the said Master John Hus had been at Constance three and a half weeks, it was rumoured through the city that Master Hus had been taken out of the city in a cart used for carrying hay; but it was false. This is how it was: the servants who rode out with the cart for hay did not take the cloth or cover, called in the vernacular a sperloch, off the cart, but only after two or three trips took it off and carried hay again without that cover. From this some then supposed, or it was rumoured, that Master Hus had already been taken out of the city in the hay, and so had escaped in the hay and been carried out; but in truth there was never any mention of such a thing, as afterwards appeared and now appears. Had he escaped, he would never have been seized and put in chains, nor in the end condemned by them.
NOTE: The story of the haycart was later told as fact: that Hus had tried to flee and so forfeited his safe conduct. Peter of Mladoňovice, a bachelor of Prague and secretary to Hus's protector Jan of Chlum, was at Constance throughout; his account is the eyewitness report of Hus's party, written soon after 1415.

## Mlad. Arrest 4 'Good words'
EN: And when he had come to the cardinals and greeted them, they said to him: Master John! Many strange things are said of you, that you have held and sown many errors in the kingdom of Bohemia; and so we had you called, wishing to speak with you, whether it is so. And he answered: Most reverend fathers! Let your paternity know that rather than hold a single error I would die. And see, I have come freely to this holy council, and if I am shown that I have erred anywhere, I am ready to be humbly corrected and to amend. And the cardinals said: Those are certainly good words. NOTE: 28 November 1414. From this day Hus was a prisoner, first in the house of a canon, then from 6 December in the Dominican convent on the island in the Rhine, later in the castle of Gottlieben. Sigismund, who reached Constance at Christmas, protested and then let the council keep him. The formula Hus uses, ready to be corrected 'if shown' his error from scripture, is the one he held to the end (Mlad. Sentence [1]).

NOTE: The burning of the coat and shoes 'lest the Bohemians have it as a relic' shows that the authorities already expected what followed: in Bohemia Hus was venerated as a martyr, and 6 July became a feast day of the Utraquist church.

## Decr. Sentence 2 'A true and manifest heretic'
EN: Having seen, moreover, the acts and proceedings had and done in the case of inquisition into heresy against the said John Hus, after a faithful and full report by the commissioners deputed in this case and by other masters in theology and doctors of both laws on the acts, proceedings and statements of very many trustworthy witnesses, which statements were read openly and publicly to the said John Hus before the fathers and prelates of this holy council, and by which it is most plainly established that the same John taught many evil, scandalous and seditious things and dangerous heresies in public and preached them for many years: calling on the name of Christ, this most holy synod of Constance, having God alone before its eyes, by this definitive sentence which it pronounces in these writings, pronounces, decrees and declares that the said John Hus was and is a true and manifest heretic,
NOTE: The appeal to Christ (Hus's appeal of October 1412 from the pope to Christ, 'the most just judge') is named in the sentence itself as an offence. The sentence rests on the witnesses' depositions; Hus had answered that the articles drawn from them were false (Letters [4]).

## Decr. Sentence 3 Degraded and left to the secular arm
EN: Therefore, for the above and much else, this holy synod pronounces that the said John Hus was and is a heretic, and judges and condemns him by these presents to be judged and condemned as a heretic; rejecting the said appeal as injurious, scandalous and a mockery of ecclesiastical jurisdiction; and declaring that the same John Hus seduced the Christian people, above all in the kingdom of Bohemia, in his public sermons and in the writings he composed, and was not a truthful preacher of the gospel of Christ to that Christian people according to the exposition of the holy doctors, but rather their seducer. And since, from what this most holy synod has seen and heard, it has recognized the same John Hus to be pertinacious and incorrigible, and so wholly so that he did not wish to return to the bosom of holy mother Church, nor to abjure the heresies and errors he had publicly defended and pre
NOTE: The cut names the archbishop of Milan and the bishops of Feltre, Asti, Alessandria, Bangor and Lavaur to carry out the degradation (Mlad. Sentence [2]). The council does not sentence to death; it 'relinquishes' the heretic, and the secular arm, here the king of the Romans, does the rest (Mlad. Sentence [3]).

## Decr. Articles 1 Articles condemned
EN: The condemned articles of John Hus, of which mention is made above. 1. There is one holy universal Church, which is the whole number of the predestined. And it follows below: The universal holy Church is only one, as there is only one number of all the predestined. [...] 7. Peter was not, and is not, the head of the holy catholic Church. [...] 9. The papal dignity grew up from the emperor, and the appointment and institution of the pope came from the emperor's power. [...] 20. If the pope is wicked, and especially if he is foreknown to damnation, then like the apostle Judas he is a devil, a thief and a son of perdition, and is not the head of the holy Church militant, since he is not even a member of it. [...] 30. No one is a civil lord, no one is a prelate, no one is a bishop, while he is in mortal sin.
NOTE: Thirty articles, most drawn from De ecclesia (1413). Hus did not deny that he had written many of them, but held that several were wrenched from their context; he had glossed them with his own hand in prison, and Mladoňovice records his answers in the cathedral. Article 30 frightened the princes as much as the prelates: a king in mortal sin would be no king. Article 20 was the charge against John XXIII made into heresy, a month after the council had deposed him.
